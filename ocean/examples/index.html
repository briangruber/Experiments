Abyssal · Three.js examples

Serve this directory (npm start) — ES modules will not load from file://.

WebGPU — the whole ocean in one call The createAbyssal() facade: FFT sea, volumetric sky, spray and HDR post, on WebGPU with WebGL2 as the automatic fallback. Presets and real cloud genera as buttons; your meshes share the frame's depth. WebGPU — drop a crate, cross two wakes abyssal.bodies.add(mesh, { mass, float, wake }): hulls float from mass vs volume, splash on impact, and write one shared wake field so crossing trails mix. A mesh you only scene.add() is still a statue. WebGPU — gravity-wave wake Drop the occupancy cut and leftover stamps. The hull only displaces water; leftover crests travel in world XZ at c = √(g L / 2π) and add. U / c is plow (circles), hull speed (stern pile-up), or a leftover Mach V. Stop or turn and the old waves stay in the water. WebGPU — a jet ski that planes The same OceanBody as a crate, with the SKI coefficients: hover (ride height), springiness, grip. The models/jet-ski.glb hull. W/S throttle, A/D steer, X holds a cruise speed, U hides the chrome. The Ski panel dials the wake trail (height, width, center cut, foam), spray (waterline sites, hull jet, amount), the sea-dragon swell (dome, bow heap, laminar mound), and a debug toggle for spray / buoyancy contact points. A second Sea panel writes abyssal.params — spray size, lifetime, hull jet, sea state. Tropical Noon is a 5–100 m bed with sand, reef, brighter floor caustics, and scattered rocks / coral from models/. Pierce lab — a fin through a calm surface The bench, not the ocean: no FFT, no sky, no spray, so it opens instantly. Drag a shark fin (or a rod) through a nearly flat sea and watch src/pierce.js — the collar of displaced water, the heap ahead, the shoulders drawn down, the hollow astern — plus the rings it sheds from src/wake-wave.js. Every reach is a multiple of the site's own size, so copy knobs gives you numbers that still work on a mesh ten times bigger. The near field button swaps that painted model for src/ripple-field.js, a simulated height field where you only displace water and a wave equation produces the hollow and the V by itself. WebGPU — a sky of real clouds, no water water: false: the atmosphere and cloud raymarch over your own ground. Cirrus, cumulus, stratus, nimbus, cumulonimbus — measured recipes, switchable live, with a sun-elevation slider. Water and sky Both components sharing one parameter set, with Three.js pilings standing in the sea. Watch the waterline move across them — that is a shared depth buffer. Water only The sea under Three's own scene.background. Shows the ordering change a background forces, and what you give up without the matching sky. Sky only Atmosphere and volumetric clouds over a scene with no water at all, with live sun-elevation and cloud-cover controls.
